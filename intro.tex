\section{Introdution}

Trying to quit a smoking addiction has been shown to be a very hard challenge. 
People could thus use some help in this process, but surveys show that very few trying to stop find the help of a professional.
In this modern age, we could seek the help of digital tools such as apps, as a more accessible and cheaper alternative.
Although a broad range of apps exists, they remain limited to progress trackers extended with a limited number of features and fixed step-by-step plans.

We want to build an app that fills this void. The app learns about the user's needs and preferences, and merges a broad range of existing features.
By using gathered data combined with expert backed behaviour change models, it dynamically chooses the right features and interventions for each user at each point in time.

This document gives an overview of the progress and state of this project,
how we arrived at the idea we have, and our vision of how we will continue to refine and turn it into reality.

Section \ref{sec:design-thinking} details the different phases we went through to arrive at our current idea.
We interviewed smokers and other possible stakeholders in the empathize phase, to define their needs in the define phase.
In the ideate phase, we worked out how we could build a smart app that can solve these needs.
In the prototype and testing phase we are working out a storyboard of the interactions with the app, a visual interface and
an idea for the theoretical framework through several iterations by gathering feedback from end users and experts.

In section \ref{sec:SOTA}, we analyse the current State Of The Art (SOTA) in the field.
Identifying our competition, what they lack, and what we can learn from them. 
We go over key research in smoking cessation apps and behaviour change theory, 
and identify usefull knowledge for this project.

Finally, in section \ref{sec:vision-board}, 
we go over the important recommendations we received from experts and users. 
And we present our current vision of the product.
