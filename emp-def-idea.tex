% Short introduction to the design thinking phase.
Our design thinking phase was far from a linear process.
Some phases were passed through multiple times/revisited.
In the following sections, the different phases will be discussed as one complete phase, which for us doesn't mean that all the described actions, decisions, etc.\ were made in the same week/period.

\subsection{Empathize phase}
\label{subsec:empathize}

% Content for the empathize phase.
In our empathize phase we decided on what our stakeholders and target personas would be.
We went through this process mainly by first having our initial meeting with our challenger Geert Crombez.
After this meeting our whole team felt like we didn't know what direction we could/should go in.
Then we had a team meeting and decided upon our target group for behaviour change, namely smokers.
By making the decision to make a smoke-stop app, we then looked for (ex-)smokers to interview and see/feel what the needs of those people are in the process to stop smoking (by using an app).
Out of those interviews, 2 main themes came back each time:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The path to stop smoking has to start and come from within the person themselves.
    \item In the process of stopping to smoke, rational arguments (e.g. health benefits or saved money) don't really make a difference.
\end{enumerate}
The first theme was also something our challenger already mentioned in our first meeting, so it became clear to us that our app would not have to convince people who don't want to quit, but help the people who already do.

The (ex-)smokers we met could be divided into three groups: people who tried to stop before and failed, people who have stopped and want to keep it that way, and people who are trying to stop for the first time.
Next to the 2 themes above, the interviews also showed us two needs they have.
First, smokers don't know which method or app suits them.
There are a lot of apps, but most of them are of low quality, and testing them one by one costs time and motivation.
On top of that, a failed attempt with the wrong app feels like a personal failure.
Second, cravings can have very different causes (e.g.\ stress, a certain place, boredom, a social situation or physical withdrawal), and the same technique doesn't work for every cause.

Although the challenge seemed pretty difficult at this stage, we decided to keep going and chose (ex-)smokers as end-users for our app.

The thing we liked most about this phase was that we felt like there were still a lot of paths we could follow and directions we could explore.
Because the interviews showed us that rational arguments don't really work, we knew we had to look further than the typical smoke-stop apps, which gave us a lot of freedom for the next phases.

\subsection{Define phase}
\label{subsec:define}

% Content for the define phase.
The main event that happened and shaped our define phase was our talk with the PhD student Arian.
He worked on research about using chatbots (with direct interventions) to help people stop smoking by detecting craving moments.
Although we now know that the outcome of their research is that sometimes getting reminded of your cravings leads to more smoking, the meeting gave us a great boost in motivation and gave us a clear vision to keep going and put our heads in the same direction.
Something else we learned from this meeting is that we would be needing a thorough SOTA to see what already has been done in this space and where our app/method could go.
Now we were ready to define our HMW question.
To do this, we clustered all the HMW questions we came up with.
The two largest clusters were ``the right method for the right person'' and ``help at the moment of the craving'', which match the two needs we found in our interviews.
Taking the meeting with Arian into account, we decided we wanted to touch on a wider range of interventions to help people stop smoking, and not only work with a chatbot.
So our define phase concluded with the following HMW question: ``How might we choose the correct (direct vs.\ indirect) kind of intervention (BCT) for the right person at the correct moment (in the day) to stop smoking?''
The clear thing we all liked about this phase of our design thinking process is that for the first time, we felt like all our heads were in the same direction.
We also could all bounce ideas off each other way more easily.

\subsection{Ideate phase}
\label{subsec:ideate}

% Content for the ideate phase.
With our HMW question in mind, we started our ideate phase by looking at all the different ways we could help people stop smoking.
Quite fast, we realised that there might be a lot of things that are possible, and that it would be impossible to work out all of them.
Taking into account what we learned from the interviews and from our meeting with Arian, we decided that a combination of direct and indirect interventions would be the way to go.
Direct interventions alone (e.g.\ a chatbot reacting on a craving moment) can sometimes do more harm than good, while indirect interventions alone might not be strong enough when someone really needs help.
By combining both, we could try to choose the right kind of intervention for the right person at the right moment, which is exactly what our HMW question asks for.
During this whole process, we always kept GDPR and privacy in mind, since our app would be working with very personal information.

Something we learned in this phase is that there are a lot of ways to get to our goal.
There are lots of media that could be used to gather personal info about a person (e.g.\ their phone usage, location or wearables), which could help us to detect the right moment for an intervention.
But maybe not always should we use all of them, because not everything that is technically possible is also allowed or desirable when it comes to the privacy of our users.
So for every idea, we not only had to ask ourselves if it would work, but also if it would be okay for the user that we use this information.

The thing we liked most about this phase was that we could explore all the options that might be available to get to our goal.
Because we didn't have to choose right away, everyone could bring in their own ideas, no matter how crazy they seemed at first, which led to a lot of interesting discussions within our team.
