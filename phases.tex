\subsection{Prototype}

To bring our idea into a testable form, we created a prototype consisting of two different parts: a storyboard that
would give an outline of how a possible user might interact with the app, and a user interface, to be able to present to possible end users what the actual concept might look like.
We need both parts, to be able to clearly explain to a possible end user what the app might look like an how it would work, in order to collect feedback.

\subsubsection{Storyboard}
Below is a step by step explanation of the flow of interaction with the app.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item A smoker comes in contact with the app
    \item After downloading the app, the user will fill in a questionnaire with imporant information, through a conversation with the chatbot.
    \item Scenario: the user is trying to stop from this day onwards (which we learned from the questionnaire).
    \item On day 1, at a certain time, using wearable and other provided data by the user, the app detects high risk of craving and sends a notification, for example, a message from the chatbot.
    \item The user might then actually interact with the notification, or ignore it.
    \item Based on the amount of interaction with the user, and possibly some new questions asked by the chatbot, the app tries to re-assess wether the user actually smoked, and if the notification was effective.
    \item If the notification was not effective, the next day, or at the next high risk moment, the app will try to send a different notification, with a different message, or at a different time. This could now be a notification for a funny news story, that might temporarily distract the user from their craving.
    \item Again, after re-assessing the success. The app learns that this intervention was effective or not.
    \item If these notifications keep failing, these craving support notifications might not be the right method for this specific user.
    \item The app should then learn this, and try a different feature, such as: helping the user plan a less stressful day, or provide them with plans to cope with craving moments without their phone.

\end{enumerate}

As a summary, this storyboard higlights our idea of the smart smoking cessation app:
It keeps learning about the user, to try and choose the right features at the right time, and adapting the specific feature to their preferences.
It combines direct, smoking cessation support, with indirect support, such as stress management and distraction techniques. Depending on what works best for the user, and what they prefer.

\subsection{Testing the prototype}

With these 2 parts of the protoype, we could go towards possible end users for feedback.
We interviewed some (ex-)smokers from our own environment, as well as non-smokers, such as peers, friends, family members.
As a non-smoker could also give interesting insights into possible problems with the app interface and flow.

We proposed our prototype by pitching our idea, explaining the flow through the storyboard, while showing the user interface.
Through this, we received a range of interesting and usefull points of feedback.

\subsubsection{Received feedback}
On the positive side:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Smokers believed in the effectivity of Just In Time Interventions, sending notifications to help them cope with cravings, direct or indirectly related to smoking.
    \item Using a chatbot is received as more pleasant than having to fill in a long questionnaire.
    \item Users trying to quit smoking would be open to share usefull data, like wearable and location information.
    \item Some users like the idea of an app that learns about them and their smoking triggers, allowing them to review this data and learn more about themselves.
\end{enumerate}

On the negative side, possible problems with our app:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Long questionnaires and chats with chatbots are not well received and should be avoided.
    \item Daily notifications, and multiple notifications throughout the day can be annoying.
    \item Without notifications, some might forget to open the app.
    \item Showing metrics such as craving risk could have an opposite effect: If a user sees a high risk of craving in their app, this can can be an extra trigger or excuse to actually light a cigarette.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Takeaways from the feedback}

Presenting this visual and interactive prototype to possible users gives very usefull
information about the implementation of different features, and the flow of our app. 
But, importantly, our app idea focuses on a framework that incorporates a large range of features, 
where we should be able to add (and remove) possible features, even after deployment.
The user personalization is based on dynamically choosing among these features, and fitting them to the user.
Therefore, due to the changing nature of our design, 
we will have to re-iterate the gathering of user feedback frequently to test how they perceive new features and implementations.
